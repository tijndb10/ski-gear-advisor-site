Before you buy: a quick checklist

  • Get boots fitted in a shop. Boots are the most important piece of gear, and fit matters more than any spec. Try a few on and walk around for 10 minutes.
  • Have bindings set by a technician. The release setting depends on your weight, height, skiing level and boot size.
  • Check ski length when you hold them. We suggest a length per ski; anything within a few centimetres is fine.
  • Not sure you will ski often? Renting skis and buying only boots is a popular, cost-effective middle ground.
  • Compare prices. Last season's models are often much cheaper and nearly identical.

Ski words explained

Waist width
How wide the ski is underfoot, in millimetres. Narrow (under 80) is quick on groomed runs. Wide (over 95) floats in deep snow.
Flex / stiffness
How much the ski or boot resists bending. Softer is easier and more forgiving. Stiffer is more stable at speed but demands more skill.
Rocker
An upward curve in the tip (and sometimes tail) of the ski that makes turning easier and helps in soft snow.
Last
The width of the boot at the forefoot, in millimetres. Narrow is about 97–99, medium about 100–101, wide 102 and up.
Walk mode
A switch on the boot that frees the cuff so walking and hiking are more comfortable.
Mondopoint
Ski boot size in centimetres of foot length. We estimate it from your normal shoe size.

These recommendations are guidance, not a guarantee of fit. Always try boots on in store.
